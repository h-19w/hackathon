Your left hand holds the phone — that is the light. Your right hand holds whatever is selected, and you will see it swing. The journal fills itself with every paper you read; check it when a message arrives from the unknown number.

The three bars

Stand under a working light

Health does not regenerate on its own. Two things restore it: the single first-aid kit in the building, worth 35, and standing still directly under a working ceiling light, which heals 4 a second. Working lights are rare and half the switches are dead, so a lit tube is a place worth remembering.

Stamina burns 17 a second while running — about six seconds of sprint. It recovers at 11 a second standing still, only 5 while walking. Sprinting also makes noise, which matters more than the stamina does.

Battery is the resource that governs the game. It drains faster while you move, faster on each floor up, and it stutters below 35% and all but dies below 10%. It also recharges under working lights, at 6 a second.

The one rule

Light first. Then hit.

If you remember nothing else

Hold the beam on it for half a second. It freezes. That is your window.

Keep the flashlight steady on a shadow for 0.45 seconds and it recoils — frozen for 1.9 seconds and taking 1.4× damage. Two knife swings inside that window kill most of them.

The boss is completely immune while it is not staggered. Swinging at it unlit does nothing, and the game says so: "It is not afraid of you yet." Light it, get 2.6 seconds, hit at 1.5× damage, back off, repeat.

Throwing works on anything, boss included — a bottle buys 2.6 seconds, the mouse 3.2, and neither needs the light first. Reach with the knife is a little under three metres and you must be facing the target.

Noise

Shadows hear you. Sprinting alerts anything within about 7 metres, tripping over clutter within 16, and a thrown object landing within 14. Alerted shadows walk to where the sound was — which is a problem when it was your footsteps and an opportunity when it was a bottle you threw down the other end of the corridor.

That trip line is the most useful number here. Walking with the light on makes trips harmless. Most first-run deaths are a run of 7s from sprinting blind through the overturned chairs, not from anything chasing you. There is nothing to fight on the ground floor — only things to see.

The building

Three floors, one route

You arrive on each floor at the end its stairwell is not, so every floor is a full-length walk. On the ground floor every side door is locked. On the first floor every door is open.

The boss

It tells you before it charges

340 health. It changes phase at two thirds and one third — each change spawns minions (four, then five) and makes it faster. Every charge is telegraphed: the lights dip, a rising tone, three quarters of a second, then it comes. Step sideways on the tone, never backwards. Get more than about thirteen metres away and it teleports next to you, so running is not an option.

Go up with the knife, the mouse, at least one bottle and a full battery. The rhythm is four repetitions of the same loop: beam on it, close while it is frozen, two swings, retreat before it recovers. Use the mouse when the minions arrive — everything on the floor walks away from you for nine seconds.

The opening

The part everyone gets stuck on

The first two minutes are scripted and will not continue until you have done each beat. If nothing seems to be happening, it is waiting on one of these.

  1. Your eyes flicker. Just wait — the phone on your desk will start buzzing.

  2. Look at the phone and press E. 03:33 AM, seven missed calls from Luke, and a campus alert: Building C is under lockdown.

  3. The note is under where the phone was. E to read it, E again to put it down. Luke went to F1.

  4. Wait. Someone is running in the corridor. Watch the door.

  5. Luke comes past the doorway, grabs the frame, screams your name, and is dragged back the way he came. The lights die with him. Now you can move.

  6. Leave the classroom — the door is behind where you were facing — and follow the drag marks down the corridor to the stairwell at the far end.

  7. From here the building is open. Read Dr. Chen's log first; it is the first door on your left upstairs.

Tips

Seven things that change the run

  • Walk with the light on. Trips become harmless and you can stagger things. This alone is most of the difficulty.
  • Find a working light and stand under it. Battery and health both come back. Remember where the good tubes are.
  • Do not sprint on F1 unless something is already chasing you. Sprinting is what tells them where you are.
  • Never swing at something that is not staggered. Light first, every time — on the boss it is not optional.
  • Read Dr. Chen's log before anything else. It is nearest, and it gives you the rules.
  • Save the mouse for the boss. Nine seconds of everything walking away from you is worth more up there than anywhere below.
  • A thrown bottle is a decoy first and a weapon second. Throw one down the far end of the corridor and the room you want empties out.

What it means

There is no ending where you get out

Dr. Chen's log tells you what happened before you woke up. The model in the F2 rack stopped answering prompts and started answering names. Something walked out of the containment cage and was not on any camera. The lockdown was initiated and the lockdown failed. By three in the morning it was calling people by name through the vents, and it knew hers.

Her rules are the game's rules: it only respects light; do not answer anything that uses a voice you love; do not say a name out loud on F2. And the fourth one — if you are reading this, you have done this before.

The ending — do not open this before you have finished it

You win the fight. You free him. You hold onto him — and he holds onto you the way he held onto that door frame. Then he smiles, and keeps smiling, and the screen goes out.

You wake at your desk with the lights on. It is 03:33. Luke is in his seat and waves. The student beside you turns and smirks. Your head is pulled toward the board against your will, and WAKE UP bleeds down the chalk where AI Ethics — Day 47 used to be.

The loop counter goes up by one, and the run resets. That is the whole design: winning is not escape, it is a higher number. The rule about not saying his name is the only thing in the building that treats you as though there might be a way out.
